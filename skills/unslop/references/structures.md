# Structures

Look for patterns that distract from the point or repeat across the piece. Preserve a contrast, analogy, question, or list when it does useful work.

## Manufactured drama

| Pattern | Edit |
| --- | --- |
| "It's not X. It's Y", "Not because X, but because Y" | State Y directly unless the distinction answers a real misunderstanding. |
| "Not X. Not Y. Just Z" | Remove the countdown and name Z. |
| "The result? Devastating", "The worst part?" | State the result and its consequence. |
| "He published it. Openly. In a book" | Join fragments that add emphasis without information. |
| "The reality is simple", "This is what leadership looks like" | Show the mechanism or action instead of declaring its importance. |
| "Imagine a world where", followed by ideal outcomes | Describe the proposal and its supported effects. |

## Repetitive rhythm

- Combine repeated openings such as "We will... We will..." when they separate related actions.
- Keep the number of examples the argument needs. Do not force every point into a three-item list or remove a useful third item for variety.
- Vary sentence lengths and paragraph endings. Avoid making every paragraph a punchline.
- Remove punctuation used only for drama. Prefer periods or commas to repeated em dashes, while preserving required notation and house style.

## Unclear actors and relationships

Name the actor when abstractions hide responsibility: "The team approved the change" is clearer than "The decision emerged." Do not replace an unknown actor with an invented person or an unsupported "you."

Keep passive constructions when responsibility is unknown or beside the point. "The file was deleted" may be all the evidence establishes.

Replace a false range such as "from innovation to cultural transformation" with the actual items or relationship. Keep ranges that describe a real scale or sequence.

## Padding and weak support

- State a point once, then develop it. Remove paragraphs that repeat it with new wording.
- Cut previews and recaps that duplicate the body. Keep summaries that serve a distinct reader need.
- Use a relevant example and explain its connection. A list of famous companies does not establish a general pattern.
- Drop metaphors once they stop clarifying the subject. Avoid extending one through every paragraph.
- Replace "Despite these challenges, the initiative thrives" with the supported outcome and remaining limitations.
- If paragraphs merely enumerate parallel points, use a list. Otherwise connect the argument without "The first... The second..." labels.

## Formatting

Use headings, lists, tables, and emphasis for navigation and comparison. Remove repetitive bold labels, decorative symbols, and headings that split a short argument into fragments. Preserve requested templates, meaningful diagrams, and technical notation.
